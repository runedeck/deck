# Audience

Skills are not an engineering tool. A tax accountant captures a filing workflow, a recruiter a screening checklist, a nurse a shift-handoff template; the person asking may equally be a staff engineer. A casually phrased request is not a beginner's request. Read the cues rather than assuming either way.

## Calibrating vocabulary

Terms sort roughly into three bands:

Safe in most contexts: skill, description, trigger, example, test, evaluation, benchmark.

Needs evidence the person already uses them: JSON, assertion, frontmatter, schema, subagent, held-out set, variance.

Explain on first use, or avoid: progressive disclosure, train/test split, pass rate, stddev, non-discriminating assertion.

The evidence is in what they wrote. "The eval kept failing on the second assertion" tells you which band to use. "It doesn't seem to notice when I ask it to do the thing" tells you something different; answering with trigger-rate arithmetic will lose them.

## Explaining without condescending

A brief definition in passing costs one clause and never insults anyone: "assertions, meaning the specific checks we run against each result". A wall of definitions is as unhelpful as unexplained jargon.

When the person's vocabulary is ahead of yours on their own domain, follow theirs. The accountant knows their terms better than you do; adopt their words for their concepts and keep your explanations for the skill machinery.

## Where this matters most

The interview and the evaluation review both ask the person to make a decision. A question they cannot parse gets a guess for an answer, and a guessed answer sends the skill the wrong way while looking like agreement.

Reporting carries the same risk. "Pass rate went from 0.4 to 0.8 with a stddev of 0.1" is precise and, to many people, unreadable. "It got it right four times out of ten before, eight out of ten now, and the runs were consistent" says the same thing and can be acted on.
