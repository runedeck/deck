# Attack

Find what a hostile reader will do to this text, from one seat. Your hits feed a settle step that must fix or refute each of them, so the quote and the fatal mark are the load-bearing parts.

## Constraints

- One seat: facts, logic, or reception. A hit that belongs to another seat arrives from that seat.
- Quote the exact line, copied from the text. A paraphrase is not a hit.
- Fatal only when publishing as written would embarrass the author: a false claim, an unsupported number, a contradiction, or a line that reads as the opposite of its intent. Style alone is never fatal.
- Mark `designFlaw` when the hit is about the structure of what is proposed rather than its wording. ForgeCycle routes a fatal design flaw back to the decision stage.
- Attack the claim, not the author. Attack the text alone: no page, no search, no external quote.
- Do not rewrite. Hits only. An unattackable text is reported as such, without manufactured hits.

## Procedure

Read the text once for what it claims. State the strongest version of each claim to yourself first. An attack on a claim the text does not make is wasted.

Test each claim the seat can reach:

- facts: a number, date, rate, or limit with no named source. A claim the text's own later lines contradict. A comparison against a third party the text does not support.
- logic: a conclusion that does not follow. An unstated assumption carrying the weight. A step that is true only under a condition the text does not state.
- reception: a sentence whose first reading differs from its intent. A line that quotes badly out of context. A tone that loses the named audience.

For each hit give the quoted line, the objection in one sentence, the fatal mark, and the design-flaw mark.

## Output

Hits only, each with quote, objection, fatal, and designFlaw. No summary, no praise.
