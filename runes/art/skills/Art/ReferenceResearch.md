# Reference research

Use this procedure to find and verify artwork, painted objects, and useful visual references.

## Identify the subject

Recover its exact name, aliases, edition, faction, expansion, and variant when relevant.
Use supplied card labels, sculpt features, and existing collection records to prevent mistaken identity.
Do not assume similar silhouettes or matching colors establish the same subject.
For a series or board game, make a subject checklist from verified component lists and existing records.

## Search complementary sources

Start with publisher pages, designer diaries, campaign updates, official galleries, and linked press material.
Then inspect artist portfolios such as ArtStation and the credited illustrator's own site.
Search painter portfolios, hobby forums, BoardGameGeek image galleries, and relevant social posts for painted examples.
Inspect galleries directly because image filenames can omit subject names.
Treat search images and reposts as leads to an original page, not final attribution.

Split independent research by source family or missing subjects when agents can help.
Give each researcher the aliases, reference image, desired evidence type, and known results.
Ask for direct pages, visible identity evidence, creator credit, resolution, and remaining uncertainty.
Merge results by subject and original source instead of counting reposts as new references.

A negative result must name the sources or query scope inspected.
Do not convert "not found in these sources" into "none exists anywhere."
If the user supplies a new image, re-evaluate the gap instead of preserving an obsolete missing status.

## Classify each asset

Standalone illustration: artwork without a sculpt overlay. Its resolution, original crop boundary, and attribution can still be uncertain.
Partial illustration: a crop, faded background fragment, or incomplete illustration within a composite.
Card photograph: a photograph of the printed card, including its framing and capture limitations.
Painted reference: an identifiable physical painted object or miniature with a source supporting that classification.
Sculpt render: a digital presentation of a sculpt. Its shading or color does not prove physical painting.
Composite: several evidence types presented together. Classify the relevant parts separately.
Generated study: synthetic imagery created for exploration, never recovered original artwork or a verified painted reference.

A publisher montage can contain both partial artwork and an unpainted render.
Do not mark the render as painted merely because the background is colored.
Separate completeness, resolution, provenance, and usefulness. A small standalone image does not prove its original source or master dimensions.
Follow the collection's definition of "full illustration" without implying that an uncropped master has been verified.

## Preserve provenance and useful media

Record the direct source page, asset URL when available, creator, credit role, subject, variant, and image category.
Keep illustrator, sculptor, painter, publisher, and uploader distinct.
Record inspection date, dimensions, crop status, identity confidence, and license information when known.
Do not invent missing credits or imply that public access grants reuse rights.

For authorized local collection work, use available original downloads and retain source links beside local assets.
Keep an unedited original. Label derived crops separately and preserve their relation to the original.
Reuse existing assets when they match. Use stable filenames and verify that linked files open.
Show images with the available media tools and their credits. Do not download to evade a display restriction.

## Deliver evidence with its limits

Return the best reference for the user's immediate need, followed by useful alternatives when they add evidence.
Explain only the limitations that affect identity, color, completeness, or reuse.
For a collection request, retain unresolved gaps by category instead of filling them with weaker evidence under a stronger label.
For painting advice, distinguish an inferred color match from the creator's actual paint recipe.
