# Technique checks

Use these checks for miniature palettes that depend on transparent paint, emitted light, or painted metal reflections.
They guide source inspection. They do not supply a universal recipe or mixing ratio.

## Evidence before bottle names

Read the relevant manufacturer application example, not only the bottle label or digital color chip.
Check the range and formulation when that distinction changes the method.
Record the undercoat, application method, and source access limits.
Inspect original demonstrations when the recommendation depends on brush loading, pooling, gradients, or light placement.
State whether you inspected the footage, selected frames, captions, or written instructions.
Do not attribute an exact recipe to a photographed painter without their documented process.
Uncalibrated images support approximate color judgments. They do not establish a physical match.

## Speedpaint and underpainting

An ordinary Speedpaint coat uses its flow to shade recesses over light preparation.
Use enough wet paint for even coverage. Remove excess pools before the coat starts to dry.
A glaze uses a controlled tint with less paint on the brush. It should not behave like a flooded recess shade.
Airbrushed transparent paint also acts as a tint. Do not transfer a brush recipe unchanged to an airbrush.
Use the medium appropriate to the paint when changing its strength and flow.
Check pastel and pale products individually because opacity can differ across a range.
White shading products need a light foundation. Do not assume that they replace opaque white.
A transparent color over uniform white does not automatically produce a chosen directional gradient.
Establish that value pattern in the preparation or specify a demonstrated additional technique.
Broad, smooth wing membranes need controlled sections and pool removal.
Painted scale recesses and smooth membrane gradients present different application problems.

Reject this unsupported shortcut because it assigns opaque coverage to a shading product:

```text
Apply Holy White over the black griffin head to make the feathers white.
```

Use this form after checking the product example:

```text
Prepare the head in opaque white. Use Holy White to shade that foundation.
Preserve the brightest feather edges and inspect a dry swatch before coating the whole head.
```

## Object source lighting

Locate the light source, its brightest visible parts, the receiving surfaces, and the forms that block the light.
Check the value pattern before choosing the color layer.
Ordinary recess shading can darken the same cavity that an OSL plan needs to keep bright.
That conflict requires a different preparation or application method.
A fluorescent ink remains a distinct material choice. A bright yellow or green Speedpaint is not an automatic substitute.
Verify the ink medium and compatibility when layering or mixing materials. Do not treat oil and acrylic products as interchangeable.
Speedpaint can contribute a controlled OSL tint when the preparation and placement support it.
Do not reject every Speedpaint use in OSL because a previous recipe was wrong.
Do not prescribe glow from bottle names, a surrounding halo in artwork, or an unseen tutorial.

For example, assigning Malignant Green and Maize Yellow to a skull does not establish a glow method.
A useful correction states the bright foundation, tint placement, occlusion, and the user's chosen fluorescent material.
It does not invent a mandatory ratio or a product-specific opacity claim.

## Non-metallic metal and actual metallics

For NMM, check the placement of dark values, reflection bands, bright edges, and wear before selecting transparent tints.
A grey coat alone does not establish metal reflections.
On a rusty cleaver, preserve readable exposed-steel reflections between corrosion patches.
Verify rust hue from application evidence. Do not coat every metal surface uniformly brown merely because the blade is rusty.
If the user requests actual metallic paint, judge that method on its own terms.
Metallic paint is not a failure merely because another miniature used NMM.

## Dilution and test language

Do not invent a fixed ratio to make advice sound reproducible.
When a source supplies a ratio, name both components and retain its paint, substrate, and method context.
For an unsourced mix, state the intended result and propose a small trial on the same preparation.
Record a successful ratio only after the user or a documented test establishes it.
Label a limited formulation test as limited. Do not convert it into a universal drying time.
